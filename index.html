About

I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Statistics at Harvard University, working with Professor Susan Murphy. My research develops statistical and machine learning methods that make digital health interventions smarter — deciding when, whom, and how to nudge, and measuring whether it worked.

I received my Ph.D. in Biostatistics and Health Data Science from Duke-NUS Medical School, advised by Professor Bibhas Chakraborty. Before that, I earned my Master's and Bachelor's degrees from Peking University.

Research Interests

  • Contextual bandits & reinforcement learningOnline decision-making algorithms that personalize treatment in real time.
  • Causal inference & longitudinal dataEstimating time-varying treatment effects from intensively collected data.
  • Design & analysis of experimentsMicro-randomized trials and adaptive experimental designs for mobile health.
  • Just-in-time adaptive interventionsDelivering the right support at the moment it is most likely to help.
